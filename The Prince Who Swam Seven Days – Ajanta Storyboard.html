Ajanta Cave 1

The Prince Who Swam Seven Days

A storyboard of the Mahajanaka Jātaka, as painted on the walls of Ajanta Cave 1, retold in six frames.

D Sai Sri HarshitRA2411030010053 · W1 · CSE Cyber Security

The brief

Turn a narrative from the Ajanta cave paintings into a visual story. 21LEM301T Indian Art Form, Assignment 4, CO2, 10 marks.

The objective

Interpret a historical and religious story and retell it in a modern visual storytelling format.

The method

Six hand-built vector frames, each with a shot type and a director’s note. Switch to Sketch view to see the line drawings beneath the colour.

Panel 1 of 6 · Wide shot, eye level

He sets sail

Mahajanaka’s father is dead and an uncle holds the throne. The young prince boards a merchant ship with a plan: sail to the golden land, earn wealth, and return to claim what is his.

Director’s note: a tiny figure under a huge sun. The ambition is big, the prince is small.

Panel 2 of 6 · Low angle, tilted

The storm

Days out, the sky breaks. Waves lift the ship and split its hull. Merchants cry out to their gods while the mast falls into the water.

Director’s note: tilt the frame. Diagonals and a lightning bolt make the viewer feel the deck slide away.

Panel 3 of 6 · Extreme wide, empty frame

Seven days alone

Every other soul is lost. Mahajanaka keeps swimming. He eats what he can and refuses to give in. Sun and moon pass over him seven times.

Director’s note: leave most of the frame empty. Stillness and scale show the length of the ordeal better than action does.

Panel 4 of 6 · Medium shot, rising angle

The goddess Manimekhala

The sea goddess sees a brave man in the water. She floats down in a halo of light, lifts him in her arms and carries him through the air to the shore at Mithila.

Director’s note: the halo is the brightest shape on the wall. Ajanta’s painters used rounded, glowing forms to show grace.

Panel 5 of 6 · Medium shot, high angle

Crowned by a chariot

Mithila’s king has died with no heir. Ministers send out the royal chariot to choose the next ruler. It stops beside a sleeping stranger on a stone slab, and Mahajanaka is crowned.

Director’s note: the wheel and the royal parasol are the two symbols that tell us a king is coming.

Panel 6 of 6 · Wide shot, split frame

One tree is bare

Years later the king passes two mango trees. The fruited one has been stripped and broken by people grabbing for the fruit. The bare one stands untouched. He understands that wanting brings ruin, leaves his palace, and walks into the forest.

Director’s note: split the frame in two. Left is wealth and the harm it draws, right is a figure who walks away from it.

Panel 01 of 06

Painted in five pigments

Ajanta’s painters told stories in continuous narration, with one hero repeated across a single wall. This storyboard cuts that wall into frames, the way a film would. Click a colour to copy it.
